## Chapter Eleven
### The Archive That Came First

The keeper asked them to leave enough time to put everything back.

It was twenty past three. Beyond his shoulder, a square of afternoon light lay on the reading-room floor. Cade had expected the remains of a monastery. He had not expected the plastic calendar, the radiator with a bucket beneath it, or the sound of someone moving chairs upstairs.

“Five,” the keeper said. “The doors close at five.”

Lucia handed him the permission letter.

“We understand.”

He read it rather than recognizing her and waving them through. Then he introduced himself as Bassi and showed them where to sign.

Jonah put his name beneath Cade’s. Miriam recorded the references they had come to examine.

R217. V/45.

Bassi looked at the second one.

“You have our number, then.”

“On the wrapper,” Miriam said.

“Good. The new descriptions don’t always keep them.”

He led them down a passage with the reading room on one side and a workroom at the far end. Through the workroom’s glazed door, Cade could see stacked boards and a trolley. Another door opened onto a small courtyard.

“Your driver can collect you at that gate,” Bassi told Lucia. “There is no room to turn at the front.”

She went with him to open the courtyard gate. Jonah watched them while he took the camera from his bag.

When Lucia returned, she laid the gate key beside Bassi’s register.

Cade put the white-bound commentary on the table. It looked less substantial here.

---

The transfer ledger was too large for one cradle. Bassi brought a second support and opened it across both.

Lucia laid their photographs of the Roman accession register beside it. Miriam added the wider photograph showing all three wrappers, with their labels and edges intact.

R216 above V/44.

R217 above V/45.

R218 above V/46.

In the Roman register, the transfer entries recorded the first and third. The middle item had a current catalog entry, but its arrival was absent from that page.

Bassi followed V/44 through the Valdara index to a dispatch sheet.

Here all three numbers appeared in sequence.

V/44 — one folder.

V/45 — one folder.

V/46 — one folder.

Below them, in the same hand, was a fourth line.

B/12 — accompanying papers to V/45. One box.

The dispatch total was four packages. The receipt pasted opposite acknowledged three.

Cade counted them again.

Miriam put her pencil beneath the box reference.

“This one doesn’t appear in our photographs.”

“It may never have been accepted,” Bassi said.

He turned to the returns column. An arrow from B/12 led to a dated entry several pages farther on. There was a shelf location, a receiving signature, and a note that the papers had remained together.

“Returned,” Lucia said.

“The box,” Miriam said. “That still leaves the unrecorded folder in Rome.”

Bassi checked the shelf location against a more recent inventory.

“Changed rooms. Same box.”

For the first time that day, Cade could point to what was missing.

“May we see it?”

Bassi took a retrieval slip from the desk.

While he was gone, Jonah photographed the whole opening before moving closer to the four entries. Miriam checked the images against the page.

“Keep the return column.”

“It’s in the next one.”

“Show me.”

He did.

She nodded and moved her hand out of the light.

---

The box had been repaired with gray cloth along one corner. Inside were thin folders, two small gatherings of older leaves, and an English typescript held with a rusted clip.

Bassi checked them against a list fixed inside the lid. An inventory made in 1984 described the typescript as four sheets of working translation. He showed Miriam the entry before removing the clip.

“That dates the description of it,” she said. “We still compare the sheets.”

He gave her a thin spatula to support the corner while he freed the paper.

The passage they wanted began halfway down the second sheet.

Cade placed the morning’s edition beside it.

In the modern book, the instruction was exact enough to make his hand remember its pulse.

*Let the unvoiced return retain the last completed saying when the common register is cleared. It is not to be counted among the witnesses unless the keeper recalls it.*

On the typescript, the sentence was shorter.

*What is kept apart may be brought again when the public book has been cleared.*

Beside *book*, someone had written *table?* in pencil. Beneath *kept apart* was a second possibility: *left unsaid*.

The next sentence said that what was brought again should not be admitted among the witnesses without the keeper’s act.

“Where’s the last completed response?” Jonah asked.

Miriam read the line above it.

“It isn’t specified here.”

“And the separate copy surviving the clear?”

“There is keeping and bringing back. This version doesn’t tell us how.”

Jonah set the open laptop between the two renderings, with his design note on its screen.

Cade saw the correspondence diminish. He had almost wanted that to hurt. Instead, relief came so quickly that he had to resist closing the modern book.

Miriam had not finished.

“The earlier English isn’t the original either.”

Bassi brought the corresponding leaf forward in its support. She compared its lines with the typescript, then went back to the beginning.

Cade waited through the scrape of a chair upstairs.

“There is a return,” she said at last. “There is something reserved or set apart. I can’t justify making those words the name of Jonah’s procedure. And I don’t see a term here that supplies ‘last completed.’”

She pointed to a faint mark beside the line. The typescript’s pencil question had a counterpart in the older commentary margin: two possible readings joined by a small branching stroke.

“The person working on this before us was uncertain too.”

“Could it be describing a machine?” Cade asked.

“The preceding paragraph describes a speaking figure. A chamber behind it, a keeper, a way of repeating what has been entrusted to it.”

“A recording?”

“Perhaps a mechanism. Perhaps someone speaking from the chamber. Perhaps an instruction that was never carried out. We have a description.”

Cade read the modern passage again. It had taken the old uncertainty and given it his laboratory’s proportions.

“The name is new,” Jonah said.

“In the witnesses in front of us,” Miriam said. “The exact functions are sharper in the new English as well.”

Jonah opened a fresh note rather than putting the laptop away.

“How did ours get there?”

No one answered him.

---

The box held a much newer folder behind the typescript.

Bassi explained that later correspondence was filed with the item it concerned. He showed them the separate list for those additions.

A request for reproductions. A receipt. Proof pages returned for checking.

The heading on the receipt named the Institute for Textual Systems.

Cade knew the name. He opened the technical appendix Kestrel had supplied and found it beneath *Research Partners*. The addresses matched.

He put a finger on each.

Lucia pulled her chair closer.

“What did they receive?”

Bassi read the receipt. “Images of the commentary and the working translation. Not the box.”

The proof pages carried the same section numbers as the morning’s edition. Their covering sheet bore a date nine days after Jonah’s prototype note.

Cade felt Jonah lean forward beside him.

“Which way did the wording travel?” Jonah asked.

“The dates allow someone to have used your note,” Cade said. “They don’t show that anyone did.”

Nor did they tell him when the words had first entered the proof. The date belonged to the covering sheet.

Miriam found the disputed paragraph. Here the three technical distinctions were already present, and the phrase *unvoiced return* was underlined.

Alongside it, a reader had written: *On what authority this precision? Earlier working version does not require it.*

The initials beneath the question were unfamiliar.

Cade checked the paragraph in his bound copy.

The wording was unchanged.

The question had disappeared.

He wanted to send Kestrel a photograph immediately. Let him put this beside the declaration in the introduction Kestrel had shown them. Let the team at the installation explain why an old description now sounded like something Jonah had designed.

He took out his phone, then stopped with the message window empty.

Kestrel was still the person arranging their access.

Cade disliked how much that fact mattered.

“We’ll save the comparison first,” he said.

Jonah photographed the proof, its covering sheet, and the matching receipt. Cade photographed them again with his own phone. This time he did not frame out the penciled objection to make the paragraph easier to read.

Miriam was looking at the next folder.

“What is that?”

“An examination,” Bassi said. “There is a translation with it.”

The older gathering contained questions and answers in different hands. Its later working translation named an examiner and described the witness by his duty at a speaking figure. Bassi kept the fragile pages on the support while Miriam read the modern sheet.

The witness said he had been given passages to learn before visitors arrived. A person stood in the chamber behind the figure. At a signal, that person supplied the response.

Cade read the account twice.

“There’s your keeper.”

Miriam did not look up.

“Keep reading.”

Farther down, the witness described returning after the visitors had gone. He said the chamber was empty. He asked the figure a question and received an answer.

*Who else heard it?*

*I cannot name another.*

*Had the words been given to you before?*

*Not as an answer to what I asked.*

Cade’s relief became harder to hold.

“He doesn’t say the words were new,” he said.

“No.”

“And we only have his account of the empty chamber.”

“Yes.”

Miriam turned the sheet far enough for Jonah to photograph it.

A note at the foot proposed that the witness had repeated a rehearsed passage without recognizing what he was doing. Someone had added a question mark. No answer followed on that page.

Cade thought of the familiar intonation in the laboratory. He could account for how a sentence might have reached a machine more easily than he could account for what hearing it had done to him.

He moved his phone so the whole note would fit.

“Get this one too.”

---

The front door sounded down the passage.

Bassi glanced at the clock. Twenty-five minutes remained before closing.

“I’m not expecting anyone.”

He stood, leaving the next sheet of the examination beneath its protective cover.

The first man entered before Bassi reached the doorway. He wore a dark jacket and carried a document case. A second man stopped just behind him, close enough to keep the door from swinging shut.

The first man looked at the open box.

“Bassi?”

“Yes.”

“Consultation is suspended.”

He put a paper on the clear end of the table.

Cade recognized the rectangular inventory heading before he saw the number inside its smaller box.

04.

Marcus had pressed the same mark beside his signature in Rome.

Bassi took the paper but did not sit down.

“Who issued this?”

“The office shown.”

“I need a name.”

“The material is to be secured pending review. All reproductions made during today’s consultation.”

The second man stepped past Bassi and looked at Jonah’s camera.

Lucia moved her permission letter onto the table beside the new paper.

“Our authorization is from this repository. You can wait while Signor Bassi checks yours.”

“We are not discussing the authorization.”

“Then we are not handing anything over,” Bassi said.

He reached for the desk telephone.

The second man put his hand on the receiver before Bassi could lift it.

For a moment no one moved.

Cade heard the radiator drip into its bucket.

The first man reached across the table for Miriam’s working folder. She held it against the wood.

“Those are my notes.”

“They concern the material.”

“That doesn’t make them yours.”

His hand closed over the folder’s edge. He pulled hard enough to drag her forearm across the table.

Jonah put the camera into his bag.

“Elias. Close the laptop.”

Cade was still looking at the stamp.

“Now.”

Cade reached across and closed it.

Lucia slipped into the passage. The second man left the telephone and stepped out after her. He moved toward the front entrance, spreading one arm across the passage.

She went the other way, toward the workroom.

He looked over his shoulder.

Jonah saw it.

He came around the end of the table and put the laptop into his open bag. The original leaf lay close to the folder the man was pulling. Miriam released her notes and caught the support before it tipped.

The man stumbled back half a step with the folder in his hand.

“Leave it,” Jonah said to her.

She had already set the support flat. Bassi lowered the protective cover over the leaf.

Cade reached toward the modern proof still lying on the table. A pencil question ran beside the paragraph.

Jonah caught his sleeve.

“We have that page.”

Cade let himself be pulled away.

As they came into the passage, the second man turned back along it to intercept them. His shoulder struck Jonah’s bag, driving it against the wall. Jonah turned sideways, kept one hand on the strap, and made room for Miriam to pass.

Lucia was holding the workroom door open.

“Here.”

Cade took Miriam’s elbow. She pulled free once she was through the doorway, but kept moving.

Behind them, Bassi shouted that he was calling the police.

The courtyard door opened against a ridge of grit. Lucia put her shoulder to it. Daylight widened around her until there was space to get through.

Jonah held the door while Bassi came after them with his mobile in his hand. Then Jonah let it close and followed the others toward the gate.

No one had brought the box.

---

Their hired van was farther down the lane. The driver had stopped beneath a tree, where there was room to turn.

Lucia waved him toward them.

At the opposite end of the courtyard wall, a woman stood beside a parked hatchback. She raised a phone as Cade came through the gate.

He saw the lens follow Jonah’s bag, then lift toward Miriam.

“Lucia.”

She looked where he was looking.

The woman lowered the phone. She did not approach the gate or call to the men inside. When the van pulled up, she stepped back so its side door would not hide her view.

Jonah took a photograph through the glass as they climbed in.

Bassi gave the police the address. He stayed beside the open van door while Lucia asked the driver to wait.

Cade’s hand hurt. He opened it and found a shallow cut where a corner of the laptop had caught his palm.

“They had Marcus’s seal,” he said.

“The same heading and number,” Miriam said.

“You saw it.”

“Yes.” Her sleeve had ridden up. There was a red line across her forearm. “I saw the paper.”

He looked back at the gate. No one had followed them into the lane.

Jonah’s phone showed a blurred image of the woman beside the car. It established that she had been there. Cade could not read the car’s plate.

“Was she with them?” he asked.

“I didn’t see her go in,” Jonah said.

Bassi finished the call. The dispatcher had told him to stay outside. They waited with him until the police arrived, and Lucia remained to show the officers the permission letter and describe what had happened.

Before the van took the others back to Rome, Jonah sent her copies of the ledger photographs and the image of the woman. Cade kept his second set on his phone.

Miriam’s handwritten notes had stayed inside.

So had the unread remainder of the examination.

---

The hotel receptionist looked at Cade’s key card and asked whether the equipment had been recovered.

“What equipment?”

“The case your colleagues came for.”

Cade put his hand on the counter.

“We haven’t sent anyone.”

She stopped turning the card between her fingers.

“They said you were at the repository.”

“Did you let them into our rooms?”

She looked toward the office behind the desk.

“The manager went upstairs with them.”

Jonah was already taking out his phone.

“Ask him to come down,” he said. “And please keep the door records.”

They waited for the manager before going up. He apologized in the lift, first in Italian, then in English. Two men had given the booking reference and all three names, then said a document case had been left behind. He had opened the rooms and allowed them to look while he answered a call from reception.

Cade asked for their names.

The manager had not taken them.

At the doorway to his room, Cade stopped.

His suitcase was on the luggage stand where he had left it. The shirts were folded. The charging cable still hung from the socket.

But the folder he had left zipped inside the case lay open on the desk.

Its sleeves were empty. The papers had been separated into small piles, turned so that each first page could be read. Beneath the bedside lamp was a second pile of blank sheets.

Jonah stood in the doorway of his own room farther along the corridor.

“Mine too.”

Miriam came back without entering hers.

“The clasp on my document wallet is broken.”

Cade took one step toward the desk.

“Wait,” Jonah said.

He lifted his phone and photographed the room from the threshold.

Cade stepped back.

Nothing he could see had been taken. That did not tell him what had been copied.

The manager asked whether they wanted different rooms.

“We need the police,” Miriam said. “Then somewhere else.”

Cade looked at the dark window above the desk. It held the reflection of the open folder and, behind him, the three of them standing outside a room that had been private that morning.

At Valdara, a man had tried to keep them from leaving with copies.

Here, someone had carefully separated the pages.

His phone still held the photographs. He could feel its weight in his pocket.

They had come back with evidence.

Downstairs, the receptionist had said the visitors knew where they were.

Cade looked again at the separated pages.

The manager was still holding the master key.
